During Your Journey with ELO

During the Experience

Navigating Your Active Project

Once your project is underway, your focus shifts from planning to execution. Use this guide to conduct research, track project progress, communicate with your partner, and respond constructively to feedback and unexpected changes.

Why Active Projects Require Adaptation

Real-world projects rarely follow a straight line. Requirements may change, team dynamics can evolve, clients may face unexpected constraints, and research can reveal information that challenges your original assumptions.

During this phase, your team should be prepared to adapt while continuing to communicate clearly with your partner. Managing changes, responding to feedback, and keeping decisions connected to human needs are important parts of working successfully in an engaged learning environment.

Phase 3: Managing and Executing in the Field

During the active phase of your project, focus on conducting research, tracking progress, maintaining communication with your project sponsor, and using feedback to improve your work.

During the Project Goals

  • Conduct fieldwork and research: Use human-centered research methods to observe users and contexts, gather information, and identify meaningful insights.
  • Keep the project on track: Break project goals into manageable milestones, assign responsibilities, and regularly review your team's progress.
  • Communicate with sponsors and clients: Provide regular updates so that partners understand the project's progress, challenges, and upcoming decisions.
  • Create feedback loops: Gather feedback from partners, instructors, teammates, and users, evaluate what you learn, and decide how feedback should influence the project.

Questions to Consider

  • Is our current work still aligned with the project's original goals?
  • What have we learned from users or stakeholders that challenges our original assumptions?
  • Are team responsibilities and deadlines still clear?
  • Does our client or partner understand our current progress?
  • What changes or challenges should we communicate to the partner?
  • What feedback have we received, and how should we respond to it?
  • Are changes to the project creating new risks or affecting the original scope?

Recommended Project Process

  1. Conduct research: Observe relevant contexts, talk with users and stakeholders, and collect information that can guide project decisions.
  2. Turn research into insights: Organize observations and research findings to identify user needs, patterns, and opportunities.
  3. Track your progress: Review milestones, responsibilities, deadlines, and project scope regularly with your team.
  4. Update your partner: Communicate progress, important findings, challenges, and upcoming decisions rather than waiting until the final deliverable.
  5. Gather and evaluate feedback: Ask for feedback throughout the project and determine which changes will improve the work while remaining aligned with project goals.
  6. Adapt when necessary: Adjust your methods, timeline, or proposed solution when new evidence or changing circumstances require it.
