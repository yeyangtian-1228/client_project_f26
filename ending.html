Ending the Experience

Completing, Reflecting, and Translating Your Impact

Finishing an engaged learning project involves more than submitting a final deliverable. Use this guide to transition your work responsibly, close relationships with your partner, evaluate your project's impact, and reflect on what your team accomplished.

Why Closing a Project Matters

A project is not complete simply because the final deliverable has been handed over. Closing the project responsibly helps ensure that your work continues to provide value after your team steps away.

A thoughtful ending also gives your team and partner an opportunity to evaluate the project, document important knowledge, and reflect on what worked and what could be improved. Clear handoffs and communication help partners understand how to maintain and use what your team created.

Ending the Experience: Responsible Offboarding and Handoff

As your project approaches completion, focus on preparing your work for transition, formally closing your relationship with the partner, and evaluating the outcomes of the experience.

Ending the Project Goals

  • Complete and transition your work: Organize code, design files, reports, research findings, documentation, and other project assets so that your partner can access and use them after the project ends.
  • Prepare a responsible handoff: Provide the documentation and information your partner needs to maintain, update, or continue using the work your team created.
  • Close the relationship appropriately: Clearly communicate that the project is ending, express appreciation for the partnership, and establish appropriate expectations and boundaries for any future communication.
  • Evaluate project impact: Compare the final outcomes with the project's original goals and gather feedback from your partner about the value and limitations of the work.

Questions to Consider

  • Have we completed the deliverables we originally agreed to provide?
  • Can our partner easily access all of the final project files and materials?
  • Does the partner understand how to use, maintain, or update our work?
  • What project knowledge should be documented before our team leaves?
  • Are there unfinished tasks or limitations that the partner needs to know about?
  • How closely did our final outcomes match the goals established at the beginning of the project?
  • What feedback does the partner have about the project and our collaboration?
  • How should we formally close our working relationship with the partner?

Recommended Closing Process

  1. Review the original project scope: Return to the goals, expectations, and deliverables established at the beginning of the project and compare them with the work your team has completed.
  2. Organize your final deliverables: Prepare project files, documentation, reports, research findings, and other materials in formats that are accessible and understandable to your partner.
  3. Prepare the handoff: Explain how the partner can access, use, maintain, or update the project's outputs after your team leaves.
  4. Communicate limitations and next steps: Be transparent about unfinished work, known limitations, and recommendations for future development.
  5. Gather partner feedback: Ask your partner to reflect on the project's outcomes, the collaboration process, and areas that could have been improved.
  6. Close the relationship: Thank your partner for their time and collaboration, confirm that the project has concluded, and clarify any expectations for future communication.
  7. Reflect on the experience: Consider what you learned about your technical skills, teamwork, professional communication, and the impact of your work.
